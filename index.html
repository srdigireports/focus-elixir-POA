How I will run it

Principle 1

MER first, ROAS second

Meta will over- and under-attribute. Shopify revenue against total spend is the number we both trust. Platform ROAS is a diagnostic, never the verdict.

Principle 2

Rules decide, not moods

Kill, hold and scale thresholds are agreed before launch and executed mechanically. No emotional pauses, no “let it run one more day”.

Principle 3

Creative is the targeting

With Advantage+ the audience is broad. The hook, angle and proof you show decide who converts. 2–3 new ads every week keeps the engine fed.

Principle 4

Fix the leak before the tap

A 10% lift in conversion rate cuts CAC by about 9% at the same CPC. Landing page and offer fixes are worth more than any bid tweak.

Principle 5

One change at a time

Every change is logged with a reason and a review date, so we always know what moved the numbers.

Principle 6

Scale only on proof

Budget rises in capped steps, and only while blended CAC and MER hold. If they slip, we roll back the last step, not the whole account.

Straight talk on the Month 1 target 420 orders at ≤ ₹550 CAC needs the full ₹2.3L spent at about ₹548 CAC from day one. With a managed ramp from ₹5,000 to ₹7,600 a day, Month 1 spend is closer to ₹1.9–2.1L, so the realistic range is 340–380 orders. I will define Month 1 success as: (a) a run-rate of 14+ orders a day by Day 21, (b) 30-day blended CAC ≤ ₹600 including the learning phase, (c) ≥ 380 orders. 420 is the upside case, and Month 2 is where it becomes the baseline. 03 · Unit economics and targets

The math that governs every decision

At ~₹900 AOV and ~₹408 contribution per order, a customer is profitable on the first order only if blended CAC is ₹408 or less. Your ₹550 target is a deliberate bet on repeat purchase. I want to see that bet in numbers before we scale.

Blended CACProfit on first orderMER (₹900 AOV)Orders at ₹2.3LRead

Repeat requirement = (CAC − ₹408) ÷ ₹408 contribution on a second order. MER here equals ₹900 ÷ CAC. It rises with repeat and returning-customer revenue, since MER counts all revenue.

Assumptions I need you to confirm in Week 0 (1) Is the ₹408 contribution after COGS, shipping, payment and COD fees, and expected RTO losses? (2) What share of orders is COD, and what is the RTO rate? (3) What repeat rate do you see at 30/60/90 days? These three numbers decide how hard we can push past ₹408 CAC.

Where AOV comes from

Pack prices are ₹399 (6), ₹680 (12) and ₹1,080 (24). A ₹900 AOV means your mix already leans to bigger packs. Every ₹100 of AOV lifts break-even CAC by roughly ₹45 at your margin structure. That makes the pack default, bundles and per-shot framing as important as any ad.

Seven angles, tested in a fixed order

You supply 8–12 UGC assets a month. I need 2–3 new ads a week, so supply is just enough with one spare. I will not produce creative, but I will brief what to shoot, and I will tell you precisely which hooks earn the next batch.

How testing works inside Advantage+

  • Order: angle first, then hook, then format, then creator. Never test four variables at once.
  • Batch: 2–3 ads a week, each from a different angle until two angles prove out. Then 1 winner iteration plus 1–2 fresh angles.
  • Cap: 8–10 live ads in the cold campaign. More than that dilutes spend and slows learning.
  • Diagnostics: hook rate (3-second views ÷ impressions) target ≥ 30%. Link CTR ≥ 1.0%. Then CPA decides.

Claims guardrails (protects the account)

Use

  • “Clinically tested” with a link to the study
  • Trial results stated exactly as published, vs placebo
  • Everyday situations (study block, 3 pm slump, long meeting)
  • Real reviews with names and context
  • One duration claim, matching the study

Avoid

  • Tobacco or cigarette comparisons
  • “No side effects”, “non-addictive”, “cures” or “treats”
  • Health-condition or personal-attribute callouts (“Are you anxious?”)
  • Before-and-after body or mental-state implications
  • Conflicting numbers across ads, page and infographics

08 · Operating rules

Kill, hold and scale rules, agreed before launch

Thresholds are set against ₹550 target CAC and tighten to ₹450 in Month 2. They use spend, not just time, so low-volume days do not trigger false kills.

Ad is workingCPA ≤ ₹550 with 3+ purchasesKeep, let it spend
Ad is a winnerCPA ≤ ₹450 with 5+ purchasesBrief 3 variations (new hooks, same angle) for next week

Runaway or broken deliveryDaily spend deviates more than 15% from plan, or a disapproval appearsSame-day fix and message to founder
Root-cause checklist when CAC drifts In this order: (1) tracking and event health, (2) site speed, checkout and payment gateway, (3) stock and delivery pincodes, (4) offer or price change, (5) creative fatigue (hook rate, frequency), (6) COD and RTO mix shift, (7) auction and seasonal CPM inflation. Most “algorithm problems” are 1–3.

13 · 30 / 60 / 90 day roadmap

Stabilise, then sharpen, then scale

Each phase has an exit test. We do not move on because the calendar says so.

Days 1–30 · Stabilise
  • Tracking, accounts and campaigns live
  • Ramp to ₹7,600 a day on rules
  • Find 2 angles that clear ₹550 CPA
  • Landing page fixes 1–3 shipped

Exit test: 14+ orders a day by Day 21, 30-day CAC ≤ ₹600, ≥ 380 orders, zero tracking gaps.

Days 31–60 · Sharpen
  • Iterate winners (hooks, formats, creators)
  • Retargeting refresh every 2 weeks
  • Test pack default and bundle framing
  • Rules tighten to ₹450 target

Exit test: CAC ≤ ₹500 for 14 days, MER ≥ 1.9, AOV trending up.

Days 61–90 · Scale
  • Step to ₹10,000+ a day on MER gates
  • New angles and creators for fatigue defence
  • Partnership or creator-whitelisted ads
  • Decide on retention and email handoff

Exit test: ₹450 CAC at higher spend, MER ≥ 2.0, repeat-rate data supports further push.
